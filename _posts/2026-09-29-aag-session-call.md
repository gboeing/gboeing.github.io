AAG session call for papers! "Research Show & Tell: What Gets You Out of Bed
in the Morning?" Rachel Franklin and I are organizing what we think will be a
fun session, which asks: why did you do this particular research project, and
what did you personally get out of it?

Rachel and I were chatting, and we agreed that conference talks usually leave
out the most interesting parts of the story. Like, what *really* attracted you
to this topic in the first place? Why did you decide to spend months or years
pursuing it? Was it even fun? Did you expect it to be? Did you want to solve a
puzzle, or create something cool, or make a real-world difference, or advance
your career with a high-visibility topic, or just figure out the answer to
something that was bugging you?

Researchers could spend their precious time and energy on lots of other
activities. Where do you find the joy, passion, satisfaction, or meaning that
gets you out of bed in the morning?

We seek "show and tell" talks grounded in a real research project at any
stage: published, ongoing, exploratory, unsuccessful, or abandoned. Show us
your research, and then tell us its otherwise invisible personal story. Purely
virtuous accounts are not required: intrinsic curiosity and passion can
coexist with pragmatic motivations such as workplace expectations, incentives,
metrics, and recognition. Abstracts should aim for a 50/50 split: half
describing the research and half telling the story of why you chose it. Your
AAG presentation should do the same. Questions you might consider:

- Why this project? Why did you decide it was worth your finite time and
  energy? (Was it an active decision?)
- What part of the work was fun, interesting, satisfying, or meaningful to
  you? (Do you care about "fun" as a research metric?)
- Was there a puzzle you desperately wanted to solve or something you wanted
  to understand, make, or change?
- What role did career incentives, publication metrics, employer expectations,
  money, recognition, or personal validation play?
- Did you start the project for one reason but discover a different one along
  the way?
- What does this project reveal about what motivates you as a researcher?

This session is not about why you became a geographer, complaints about
academic life, or an invitation to tack a personal anecdote onto a
conventional research talk. We want to celebrate the joy of research by
sharing a different side of our usual research stories and learning about the
many ways our colleagues find meaning in their work.

How to submit: submit your abstract to AAG by its 15 October deadline. Then
email your abstract and AAG PIN to both Rachel (rachel_franklin at cga dot
harvard dot edu) and Geoff (boeing at usc dot edu) by 20 October. Session
participants will be notified by 5 November. The AAG conference will be in
New York, 8-12 February 2027.
